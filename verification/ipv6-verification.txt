========================================
4. FINAL HSRPv2 DESIGN
========================================

The original HSRP implementation used inconsistent group numbering
between IPv4 and IPv6 and did not consistently align the IPv4 and
IPv6 Active gateway with the STP root.

The HSRP design was rebuilt using a predictable dual-stack
convention.

Final convention:

IPv4 HSRP group = VLAN ID

IPv6 HSRP group = VLAN ID + 100


Final HSRP design:

VLAN 10
IPv4 group: 10
IPv6 group: 110
Active: DSW1
Standby: DSW2
STP Root: DSW1


VLAN 20
IPv4 group: 20
IPv6 group: 120
Active: DSW2
Standby: DSW1
STP Root: DSW2


VLAN 30
IPv4 group: 30
IPv6 group: 130
Active: DSW1
Standby: DSW2
STP Root: DSW1


VLAN 99
IPv4 group: 99
IPv6 group: 199
Active: DSW2
Standby: DSW1
STP Root: DSW2


This provides a predictable relationship between:

- VLAN number
- IPv4 HSRP group
- IPv6 HSRP group
- Active default gateway
- STP root bridge

========================================
9. TROUBLESHOOTING FINDING
========================================

During the initial IPv6 HSRP verification, inter-VLAN forwarding
failed for some hosts.

The initial configuration used different and inconsistent HSRP
group numbering between VLANs and address families.

Examples from the original configuration included:

VLAN 10
IPv4 group 1
IPv6 group 2

VLAN 20
IPv4 group 2
IPv6 group 5

VLAN 30
IPv4 group 3
IPv6 group 2

VLAN 99
IPv4 group 4
IPv6 group 2


The IPv4 and IPv6 Active gateway placement also differed on some
VLANs.

An initial troubleshooting hypothesis considered whether the
behavior represented an IOSvL2/GNS3 HSRPv2 IPv6 forwarding
limitation.

That hypothesis was disproved through controlled testing.


VLAN 10 was rebuilt first using:

IPv4 group 10
IPv6 group 110
Active DSW1


After rebuilding VLAN 10, a VLAN 10 host successfully reached:

2001:DB8:20::2

with:

5 packets transmitted
5 packets received
0% packet loss


VLAN 20 was then rebuilt using:

IPv4 group 20
IPv6 group 120
Active DSW2


After rebuilding VLAN 20, a VLAN 20 host successfully reached:

2001:DB8:10::1

with:

5 packets transmitted
5 packets received
0% packet loss


VLANs 30 and 99 were subsequently rebuilt using the same convention.

Final inter-VLAN testing across all four VLANs succeeded.


Conclusion from troubleshooting:

The observed forwarding problem was NOT an inherent IOSvL2/GNS3
HSRPv2 IPv6 forwarding limitation.

The final standardized HSRP design restored successful IPv6
inter-VLAN forwarding.
